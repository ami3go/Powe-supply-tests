# Automotive Power Supply Validation Test Matrix

_Last updated: 2026-09-20 · Review audit merged with N6700/N6775A/N6791A bench mapping_

## Scope

This matrix is intended for validation of an automotive direct-current power supply, direct-current converter, or electronic control unit power stage. The available bench includes a **Keysight N6700 modular power system fitted with an N6775A source module and an N6791A electronic-load module**, plus a digital oscilloscope and thermal chamber.

It combines:

- converter characterization;
- control-loop, hot-plug, component-stress and other design-verification tests;
- automotive battery and supply disturbances;
- protection and fault-injection tests;
- conducted-transient, electromagnetic-compatibility and electrostatic-discharge tests;
- thermal, environmental and endurance tests;
- 48-volt and voltage-class-B references where applicable;
- functional-safety verification when the power supply is safety-related.

> **Important:** this is a planning matrix, not a substitute for the licensed standard or the customer/vehicle-manufacturer component requirement specification. Exact severity, waveform, source impedance, repetitions, operating mode, functional-status requirement, sample count and acceptance limits shall be taken from the applicable contract specification and standard edition.

> **Test conditions:** ISO 16750-2:2023 uses the operating modes and functional status classes defined by ISO 16750-1 and calls for at least two devices under test for validation. ISO 21780 uses functional-status levels for 48-volt systems. LV 124/VW 80000 variants and editions differ between vehicle manufacturers; use the edition cited by the customer contract. Record operating mode, required functional status and sample count for every executed test, and perform pre-/post-stress parameter checks to detect latent damage.

## Installed N6700 hardware and limitations

- **Keysight N6775A:** programmable direct-current source, 0 to 60 volts, up to 5 amperes and 300 watts. It supports remote sensing, list/arbitrary-waveform operation, adjustable slew rate and integrated voltage/current measurement. It is non-regenerative and cannot directly generate negative voltage. Its current measurement resolution is 3 milliamperes, so it is not suitable by itself for microampere sleep-current validation.
- **Keysight N6791A:** programmable direct-current electronic load, up to 60 volts, 20 amperes and 100 watts. It supports constant-current, constant-voltage, constant-resistance and constant-power modes, adjustable slew rate, list/arbitrary-waveform operation and synchronized voltage/current digitization.
- **Electrical logging:** use the N6700 measurement system and continuously retrieve measurements to the control computer. A separate general-purpose electrical data logger is normally unnecessary. Temperature, humidity, vibration and other environmental channels still require their own sensors/acquisition when the chamber or test system does not provide them.
- **Precision measurement:** retain a calibrated precision digital multimeter or power analyzer when the required uncertainty is tighter than the N6700 module specifications, when independent verification is required, or when measuring circuit nodes not connected to the N6700 source/load terminals.
- **Fast events:** retain the external digital oscilloscope and appropriate voltage/current probes for switching ripple, ringing, inrush, protection transitions, micro-interruptions and automotive transients.

### Present setup status

- **Available** — the installed N6700 modules, digital oscilloscope and thermal chamber can normally execute the test within their voltage/current/power/dynamic limits.
- **Partial** — the N6700 bench is useful for source, load or measurement, but an additional fixture or instrument is required.
- **Additional equipment required** — dedicated transient, electromagnetic-compatibility, environmental, insulation, high-voltage or other specialized equipment is required.

The **Equipment required** column lists the complete practical setup. The **N6700 mapping** column explains specifically how the installed N6775A and N6791A are used and where they cannot replace specialized test equipment.

### ISO 26262 ASIL applicability

The four ISO 26262 columns map each bench test to functional-safety applicability. **Mandatory\*** means the test is mandatory when it verifies an allocated safety requirement, fault reaction, safe-state behavior or safety mechanism. **Conditional\*** means it becomes mandatory when the item-specific safety analysis, technical safety concept, hardware safety requirements, dependent-failure analysis or operating environment makes that stress/failure mode safety-relevant. **Not ASIL-specific** means ISO 26262 does not mandate that test solely because of ASIL, although another automotive standard or customer requirement may mandate it.

The values are intentionally often identical across ASIL A through ASIL D: ISO 26262 scales verification rigor, coverage, independence and evidence with ASIL rather than defining four progressively larger fixed electrical bench-test lists. See [`iso26262-asil-test-mapping.md`](iso26262-asil-test-mapping.md) for the full rationale.

## Recommended execution order

1. Baseline characterization: PS-001 through PS-007, PS-026 and PS-031.
2. Stability and impedance: PS-021 through PS-023 and PS-025.
3. Dynamic behavior: PS-024, then PS-008 through PS-013, PS-027 and PS-028.
4. Protection/fault injection: PS-014 through PS-020 and PS-030, while PS-029 component-stress capture runs during severe tests.
5. Automotive supply profiles: AUTO-001 through AUTO-018, beginning with non-destructive ramps before severe transients.
6. Temperature corners: TH-001 through TH-005, TH-008 and TH-009, repeating critical electrical tests.
7. Endurance: LIFE-001 through LIFE-004 after short-duration validation passes.
8. Electromagnetic-compatibility pre-compliance: EMC-006 as soon as the layout and harness are representative.
9. Formal qualification: conducted transients, electromagnetic compatibility, electrostatic discharge, insulation/dielectric, environmental, 48-volt/high-voltage and safety tests with the required compliant equipment.

## Key reference notes

- ISO 16750-2:2023 is the primary low-voltage automotive electrical-load reference. Relevant clauses used here include §4.2 direct-current supply, §4.3 overvoltage/jump start, §4.4 superimposed alternating voltage, §4.5 slow decrease/increase, §4.6 interruptions/reset/starting/load dump, §4.7 reversed voltage, §4.8 ground offset, §4.9 open circuits, §4.10 short circuit/overload, §4.11 withstand voltage and §4.12 insulation resistance.
- Load dump is normatively handled by ISO 16750-2:2023 §4.6.4 and Annex A. ISO 7637-2:2011 is retained for conducted-transient methods/background; pulses 5a/5b and pulse 4 were removed from the 2011 edition.
- ISO 16750-2:2023 extends the superimposed alternating-voltage test through 200 kilohertz; the installed N6775A cannot reproduce that full range.
- LV 124/VW 80000 derivatives and editions differ. The customer contract controls the applicable edition and severity. The 2021 family includes E-23 equalizing currents and E-24 on/off duration.
- ISO 16750-4:2023 §5.8 is the dedicated condensation test. Thermal derating itself remains a product/component design-verification requirement rather than a defined ISO 16750-4 derating method.
- ISO 21498-2:2024 covers voltage-class-B electrical tests but excludes electrical safety; use ISO 6469-3 and the applicable vehicle-manufacturer high-voltage specification for isolation/discharge/interlock safety.
- Electromagnetic-emissions pre-compliance margins such as 6 decibels are project targets, not CISPR 25 requirements.
